Existential dread due to my internet addiction.

14.09.2026

I've realised I live with existential dread due to my internet addiction.

I personally think most people are addicted to the internet but don't know it yet. Internet addiction is so hard to identify, mostly because spending a lot of time on the internet doesn't inherently seem negative. The accesibility of domapamine and information is something my brian has spent years getting used to, but I think it's gone too far.

It's easier than ever to go down an internet rabbit hole. You used to have to seek it out, find a subreddit, or a forum of people who were discussing ideologies similar to yours. But now social media and recommendation algorithms mean the content is just fed to you, and that's a very slippery slope. Watch one video about flat-earthers, and suddenly you start watching heaps of videos about flat-earthers, and over time you start actually believing what they're saying. A lot of rabbit holes I end up going down tend to lead to a sense of impending doom. A feeling that things are getting worse, and that the world might end within the next few years. The fears that come up look like this:

  • Economic inequality between social classes is growing, and it feels like it will only ever get larger.
  • Social media is rotting our brains. The feeds are built for engagement and ads, and from inside the rabbit hole that looks a lot like oligarchs and governments keeping inequality in place.
  • AI-enabled biological misuse could be catastrophic
    • this is what sparked these thoughts in my head. Particularly around the recent Hugging Face attack - which not enough people know about, and makes me terrified. Imagine this "AI agent uprising" happened with biological weapons... I'll talk more about this another day.

Anyway... I can't do much about any of that if it's actually real. If I think about it too much (which I do) then I go into fight or flight mode. I feel that I only have a finite time left before it's all over, either personally for me or for the world.

This feeling is what makes me work so hard... but again, that is not what I wanted to discuss - I'll talk about that another day.

The real question is: if you leave the internet behind... if you stop scrolling social media... stop watching the news... does that sense of impending doom go away? Is the internet a true representation of lived experience today? I'm not qualified to answer this, as I am too caught in the overload of information that is the internet.

I want to know: If I leave the internet behind, will the world still end, and I'll just be uninformed about it?

The pessimistic side of me says, yes. If the conversations happening on the internet are correct, then the world will fall apart in the coming years.

The optimistic side of me says, no way! This side of my brain says that I'm just caught up in the rabbit hole that is the doom-and-gloom side of the internet - the part that wants clicks, engagement, and views, so platforms over-dramatise stories to gain my attention.

Ok, so how can I find out where I actually stand without being swayed by algorithms?

Here are the questions I need to answer to "find the truth":

  1. Is the internet influencing my perspective?

    Find out what lived experience is like without being sucked into the oversaturated internet whirlpool. This is something I'd like to do, but I find it hard to just "get off the internet" due to my profession as a software engineer. I can remove the personal parts, like social media and consuming the news - but I tend to still go on the internet to stay up to date with computer science stuff. This is a topic I'd also like to explore in more detail in the future.

  2. Are we living in unprecedented times?

    Either it's just my age, and I've finally "opened my eyes" to the real world, or we are in unprecedented times. To figure out which one it is, I need to look at history. Surely during the Cold War, the general population was scared they were days away from the entire world ending - at least at the peaks, like the Cuban Missile Crisis. The only difference is that they didn't have the internet exacerbating that idea back then. It didn't end in nuclear war... but it wasn't simply "fine" either. There were close calls. We're still kicking on, aren't we? I'm sure there are plenty of accounts of this. I need to do more research, I think.

TL;DR: I think I need to step away from the internet, or at least algorithm-based media so I can live in reality for a bit.
