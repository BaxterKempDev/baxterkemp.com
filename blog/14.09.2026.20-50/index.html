Existential dread due to my internet addiction.

14.09.2026

I've realised I live with existential dread due to my internet addiction.

I personally think most people are addicted to the internet but don't know it yet. Internet addiction is so hard to identify, mostly because spending a lot of time on the internet doesn't inherently seem negative. It's just dopamine, right? How bad can it be? I think the internet has slowly trained us to expect high levels of dopamine, both the good and the bad. I worry that I can sometimes receive so much dopamine from negative content that the negativity itself becomes addictive.

The accessibility of information (both positive and negative), and the dopamine it provides, is something my brain has spent years getting used to, but I think it's time to come back to reality.

It's easier than ever to go down an internet rabbit hole. We used to have to seek it out, find a subreddit, or a forum of people who were discussing ideologies similar to our own. But now social media and recommendation algorithms mean the content is just fed to you, and that's a very slippery slope. Watch one video about flat-earthers, and suddenly you start watching heaps of videos about flat-earthers, and over time you start actually believing what they're saying.

A lot of rabbit holes I end up going down tend to lead to a sense of impending doom. A feeling that things are getting worse, and that the world might end within the next few years. The fears that come up look like this:

  • Economic inequality between social classes is growing, and it feels like it will only ever get larger.
  • Social media is rotting our brains. The feeds are built for engagement and ads, and from inside the rabbit hole that looks a lot like oligarchs and governments keeping inequality in place.
  • AI will kill us all.
    • This is what sparked this larger discussion in my head. Particularly around the recent Hugging Face attack - which not enough people know about, and which makes me terrified. Imagine the same "AI agent uprising" was focused on creating biological weapons... I'll talk more about this another day.

Anyway... I can't do much about any of that if it's really happening. I feel like I'm living in a constant state of fight or flight. I feel that I only have a finite time left before it's all over, either personally for me or for the world.

This feeling is what makes me work so hard... but again, that is not what I wanted to discuss - I'll talk about that another day.

The real question is:

if you leave the internet behind...
if you stop scrolling social media...
if you stop watching the news...

Does that sense of impending doom go away?
Is the internet a true representation of the lived experience today?
If I leave the internet behind, will the world still end, but I just won't know it's coming?

The pessimistic side of me says, yes. If the conversations happening on the internet are correct, then the world will fall apart in the coming years.

The optimistic side of me says, no way! My inner optimist says that I'm just caught up in the rabbit hole that is the doom-and-gloom side of the internet - the part of the internet that wants clicks, engagement, and views, so it pumps my algorithms with negative stories to get my attention.

Ok, so how can I find out where I actually stand without being swayed by algorithms?

Here are the questions I need to answer to "find the truth":

  1. Is the internet influencing my perspective?

    Find out what lived experience is like without being sucked into the oversaturated internet whirlpool. This is something I'd like to do, but I find it hard to just "get off the internet" due to my profession as a software engineer. I can remove the personal parts, like social media and the news - but I tend to still go on the internet to stay up to date with computer science stuff. This is a topic I'd also like to explore in more detail in the future.

  2. Are we living in unprecedented times?

    Either it's just my current stage of life, and I've finally "opened my eyes" to the real world, or are we actually living in unprecedented times? To figure out which one it is, I need to look at the history books. Surely during the Cold War there was a general fear that the world was going to end in the near future. The only difference is that they didn't have the internet amplifying that fear back then. How did that all turn out? We're still kicking on, aren't we? I'm sure there are plenty of similar incidents of this type of wide spread fear throughout history. I need to do more research on this.

TL;DR: I think I need to step away from the internet, or at least from algorithm-based media, so I can live in reality for a bit.
